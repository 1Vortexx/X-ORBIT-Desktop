# LONG HAUL - THE RETURN TRIP

Complete campaign narrative script, first full draft

Prepared 19 November 2026. Fictional narrative. Mature language at selected emotional and danger beats.


## Scope of this edition

This draft scripts the complete 40-mission campaign from arrival through all ending combinations. Each mission has playable objectives, completion and recovery rules, and dialogue with performance directions. Branch lines are recording alternatives, not a sequence an actor should read continuously in a finished scene.

The 42-hour target concerns playable driving, maintenance, delivery, investigation and route development. It is an estimate requiring level design and playtesting. It does not mean 42 hours of spoken audio. This document is a narrative implementation draft, not evidence that the missions already exist in the game.

The separate actor files contain every spoken line in this edition with the same IDs as the master, cue lines and scene context. UI instructions, sound effects and stage directions are not voice-over. The two Mile 86 documentary episodes are separate productions and are not counted as campaign missions or recording time here.

The audition scenes previously supplied were casting material. Their final counterparts in this master supersede their wording. Record from this edition. A phrase repeated in multiple missions may use one approved take if its performance direction and source are identical.


## Story premise

Kit Vale, a mechanic around thirty, returns to settle the estate of her mother June. The garage owes money and contains unfinished customer work. Kit intends to repair what she must, sell the property and leave.

Alma Reyes wants the front door June removed from her home twelve years earlier. The door, with family height marks on its jamb, has been June's workbench. Returning it brings Kit into a community still living with an evacuation officially described as orderly and complete.

A weigh ticket, salvage ledger and two radio recordings establish that a statement about an empty truck bed became a declaration that Mercy Cut contained no residents. June knew people had returned, tried to collect them, became stranded, and later signed completion without correcting the attached vacancy record. Nico rescued the final family. Owen authorized clearance and later chose to leave the discrepancy uncorrected.

Kit returns possessions and establishes an accurate record. She also learns whether she wants to operate, share or hand over a passenger service linking the basin. The ending separates public truth from private testimony and inherited obligations from promises Kit makes herself.


## Fixed backstory and recording chronology

Twelve years before play: Mercy Basin loses mine and freight work. A flood damages Mercy Cut's bridge, wells and buildings. Initial temporary evacuation becomes permanent clearance. Notices are inconsistent. People return for possessions and pets.

June accepts a paid salvage contract. She also promises private returns. Some possessions enter county-controlled auction stock; some June buys or retains for delivery. The later household auction is separate from clearance day.

Clearance day 13:42: a net-cargo weigh ticket records 1,860 pounds on June's truck. Gross and tare are printed separately. It describes a loaded household-goods run before the famous radio exchange; it does not prove occupancy at 14:07.

14:07: after unloading, Tom asks, 'Mercy truck, how's your bed?' June answers, 'Mercy empty. Returning to depot.' The transcript is subsequently categorized as a vacancy confirmation. 'Returning' is June's colloquial route call, not a certified population count.

14:11: Tom explicitly corrects empty vehicle versus empty settlement and reports returned residents. Owen requests site confirmation. No confirmed on-site check is completed.

14:14: Owen allows the clearance instruction to stand. He remains responsible even though he expected June's return to resolve the uncertainty.

14:18: Tom reaches June about the Torres family at the east tank. June tells him to hold the crew. His next transmission is masked by engine noise. The tape proves she heard the report about the family.

Approximately 14:22: the service-road shoulder gives beneath June's vehicle. Nico reaches Rosa and Mateo by a different approach around 14:26 and brings them out. Demolition elsewhere has begun and belongings are already being lost. There are no deaths attributed to this final incident.

Later that afternoon: Ellis assists in truck recovery. At 17:10 June signs the contractor completion document with the faulty vacancy attachment uncorrected. She is paid and keeps the garage open. She has opportunities to correct it later and does not.

Following years: some objects are returned; some are sold; some remain labelled in the garage. The yellow door becomes a workbench. June avoids the conversations needed to finish her deliveries.

Week before campaign: June buys a replacement battery. Her unsent message is domestic and incomplete. She dies of an off-screen medical event, not a secret murder. The campaign never turns her death into a conspiracy.


## Calendar and geography

Kit arrives on Monday. Chapter 2 ends on the first Saturday. Chapters 3-8 span several weeks, with free rest and player-selected departure times. Chapter 9 prepares a different Saturday for the service trial. Journal dates advance between milestones; nobody has to neglect sleep to beat a story deadline.

Mercy Basin is a fictional arid inland region. Vale Garage is the home base. Mercy Motel houses displaced residents. The camp, weigh station, builders' merchant, auction barn, checkpoint, radio station and Torres home are connected by an authored highway and detours. Calder sits on a town spur. The remote relay and failed service road require expedition preparation.

Route distances in missions are level-design targets, not claims about the current procedural seed. Campaign needs a fixed network or authored seed with stable sites. Script destinations must not be silently replaced by arbitrary generated names.

## Recording and language

Record clean voice without radio, telephone, tape hiss, reverb or compression effects. Sound design adds them later. Preserve room for an interruption without swallowing the required words.

Default accent is the actor's natural, mutually compatible regional English. No caricature accent or mandatory ethnicity is assigned by a surname.

Playing age is the character's age, not a restriction on the performer's age or gender. Cast by performance, with final creative approval before changing character identity.

Profanity belongs to Kit's loss of control, the checkpoint danger and Owen's defensive crack. Do not add improvised swearing to every sentence. No slurs. Battle barks have cooldowns and are not played repeatedly over intimate scenes.

Square brackets after the speaker are acting directions and branch conditions. Record all allocated alternatives. Actor IDs are unique per occurrence; the delivery manifest preserves scene order and previous-line cues.

A slash in a condition means either condition selects the line. Shared lines play after the chosen branch. For a condition not met, omit the line entirely; do not read the condition.


## Campaign state and recovery contract

Every mission saves at acceptance, safe arrival, completed repair/evidence step and ending. Scene flags are written before rewards or item transfer. Reload never awards a fare twice or duplicates an archival item.

Critical evidence is copied to Kit's office archive before the next dangerous journey. A destroyed vehicle cannot erase the only copy or permanently strand the story.

A combat death reloads the latest safe campaign checkpoint. No forced loss of a named character is required for progression. If a system permits incapacitating Sera, Len provides the written road branch. Core witnesses are protected against accidental permanent narrative deletion.

Supplies lost in 05.01 have explicit replacement sources. Low cash has specified labour or loan alternatives. None requires repetitive money farming.

Surrender and toll outcomes use existing game systems, with campaign-specific safe capture recovery. No mission requires harming a noncombatant.

All narrative timers use playable windows. Prompts, transcripts and accessible waveform tools preserve evidence for deaf/hard-of-hearing players. Route timing allows paused dialogue and menu use.

Player choices do not change historical events. They change trust, cooperation, resources, consent, service outcome and publication.


## Ending resolver

Resolve service intent and trial assessment separately. RUN, COOPERATE, HANDOVER and LEAVE_UNARRANGED each combine with PASS, CONDITIONAL or REVISE.

PUBLIC_REDACTED, FAMILY_CONTROLLED and PRIVATE_CORRECTION combine with every service result. The final chapter scripts all selected modules; do not force every modular combination into a separately recorded speech.

No public branch overrides Rosa's address redaction or Mateo's refusal. Both the original and correction remain in the administrative record.

The leaving endings finish the story before free roam. Free roam can depict Kit's remaining handover days without contradicting her planned departure.
