# LONG HAUL - THE WAY BACK
## Production bible - edition 2.0

X-ORBIT Studios / 22 November 2026. This edition replaces The Return Trip as the intended campaign story. It does not claim the replacement is already playable. The older game modules and recordings remain a separate legacy implementation until explicitly migrated.

This is a complete principal narrative and recording draft: every mission has an entry, development and resolution scene, with additional finale scenes and all four endings. Travel, repairs, survival, exploration, faction work and encounters carry the playable hours. The 43-hour route budget is a design estimate awaiting playtests; it is not 43 hours of spoken dialogue or compulsory waiting. Optional systemic speech is included after the missions. Ambient crowd improvisation and nonverbal efforts are directed separately from locked spoken lines.

## The story in one movement

In 2096, five years after the collapse, Callum Reed makes deliveries across a desert held together by settlements and contested roads. Tay and TayJ are his closest crew. Riped is an old friend whose arrogance Cal tolerates more than enjoys. Cal believes Grevyn, his closest school friend and former fellow Gucki Trooper, died after their bitter last conversation during the evacuation. Then a radio carries her real voice: Cal. Save me the window seat.

The phrase once belonged to ninth-grade history class. Later it meant that she was in danger and could not speak freely. Cal is frightened by the message and by his own hope. He does not have to forgive her to check whether she is alive. A supernatural presence along the road says it carried her words. It urges him toward her, but cannot demand his trust.

The road leads through useful community work, not a parade of people existing only to supply a rescue. Cal repairs access, transports medicine and encounters survivors of operations he helped perform. He was not the informer who betrayed Grevyn, but his service complicity is real. Evidence eventually separates those responsibilities instead of declaring him innocent of everything.

Grevyn exposed torture, unlawful detention and fascist repression within the Scatterballasian Regime. Grandpa Gucki rules it through Gucki Troopers and a knowing bargain with a predatory supernatural entity, called the Dominion in this production draft. Its food is fear and obedience. After the collapse, resistance leaders who once genuinely opposed the regime adopted its experiments. Grevyn sought their protection and was confined and deliberately exposed instead. Later cooperation with regime remnants made her a conditioned instrument for rebuilding the state she tried to expose.

Gucki Paste sustains her damaged body, regulates dangerous abilities and reinforces the controlling bond. Simply stopping it weakens control but produces unstable residual power and physical collapse. During lucid captivity she devised Golghis Paste, a substitute that supports her body while sharply weakening the bond. It is fictional supernatural medicine: the script never supplies a real preparation method. Her recovered work, corrected pages, a rescued specialist, protected supplies and an activated catalyst make a planned rescue possible. Cal's team completes her work rather than inventing her salvation without her.

The Road Force helped the resistance contact the Dominion in return for freedom from regime containment. It did not intend the experiments, but knowingly offered dangerous contact and stopped asking questions because it wanted out. It discovered the abuse while it happened, tried unsuccessfully to stop it and later guided Cal to Grevyn. Its genuine help does not erase its responsibility. Cal learns this before committing to the final rescue; Grevyn learns it before consenting to treatment. Each physical intervention weakens the Road Force. Equipment and visible recovery opportunities can compensate for early resource costs.

Riped betrayed Grevyn because he resented the trust she shared with Cal. He overheard their private evacuation conversation, used Cal's service identity, altered a protective request and supplied the private details. He planted a real handwritten note in a false context, cut real radio speech into apparent agreement and falsified payment. He let Grevyn hate Cal and told Cal she was dead, while knowing she lived. His 2096 companionship lets him monitor and redirect the search.

At an allegedly abandoned government archive, Cal and Riped are captured. Voss releases Riped under a cooperation agreement, then stages a blast in his empty cell. Cal finds the breach during his escape and reasonably believes Riped got out. Cal returns for Sable and other prisoners. Riped instead reaches Grevyn first and tells her Cal is coming to hurt her.

At the reunion, Grevyn initially rejects the paired documents, full radio log, false payment and rescue chronology. Cal shows his healed gunshot wound with consent; the scar supports independently dated records, rather than proving the entire conspiracy by itself. Restored release orders corner Riped. Believing Grevyn cannot hear, he names the private details and boasts about the betrayal. She hears him. She rises amid lightning, seizes him with the field and slams him to the floor, killing him instantly. Then the surge leaves her; she falls, severely injures herself and briefly wakes as herself. She and Cal share an honest, unfinished moment before the Dominion takes control again.

Cal survives her controlled attack by disabling reinforcement equipment, not by killing her. The crew extracts her, stabilizes her injuries and drives to the old site where the Road Force was imprisoned. They have removed its coercive locks and rebuilt guidance and medical systems separately. The final decision belongs to informed people, not a hidden relationship score.

A properly prepared severing lets Grevyn survive deterministically. A knowingly chosen emergency attempt alone involves a disclosed chance. Cal can also voluntarily take the connection into himself so the Road Force can destroy it, killing him as well as consuming itself. This sacrifice is not owed, is not proof of friendship and is not a cure for depression. He explicitly wants to live; both-survive preparation remains available. The Road Force is consumed by every completed severing, even the attempt in which Grevyn's body fails.

After the rescue sequence, Grandpa tries to destroy the Dominion to reclaim control. It kills him, but his rupture rig fatally damages it. Over subsequent days it dies out. His action does not redeem his bargain, reverse an earlier death or make the rescue unnecessary. Regime remnants, damaged communities and ordinary work remain.

## Timeline that must stay consistent

- 2085: approximately fifteen-year-old Cal meets Grevyn in ninth-grade history. Orin is their teacher.
- Late freshman year 2086: they become close; Tay, TayJ and Riped belong to the school group.
- Early sophomore year 2086: the friendship becomes central. The ordinary window-seat phrase is established.
- 2089: graduation, roughly nineteen. Cal, Grevyn and Riped enter Gucki Trooper service.
- 2091: approximately twenty-one. They uncover repression; Riped frames Cal. Collapse, evacuation, failed return, gunshot, rescue and last call occur.
- 2091-2096: Grevyn's refuge, captivity, experiments, resistance corruption and conditioning. Cal recovers and remains in contact with Tay and TayJ. Riped maintains the death story.
- 2096: main campaign, Cal and the school friends approximately twenty-six. The road journey lasts story weeks; the epilogues include later weeks and months.

## The frame-up: exact private details

In one continuous private conversation, overheard by Riped, Grevyn gives Cal three details. Her originals are behind the back panel of the map cabinet in their former history classroom. Save me the window seat now means danger and inability to speak freely. Her extraction destination is Drywell Pump Station, western loading canopy, approached by the service road because the main entrance is watched.

Cal sends a protective pickup request without naming her. Riped uses the shared service-session token after Cal leaves, preserves the signature but changes the request into a named detention order, supplies the private details and edits real radio transmissions. A genuine note asking a station to hold a civilian patient until a vehicle arrives is detached from that patient's sheet. A payment and evacuation voucher is forged using another cleared account reference.

Cal's first route follows Riped's bad guidance and reaches a destroyed crossing. Cal turns back toward the actual western service approach. Troopers block it and shoot him. Tay pulls him to cover and arranges care; TayJ drives extraction, uses contacts and keeps route and radio records. Their joint work secures treatment, a workshop refuge and recovery. One short call reaches Grevyn. She believes he never came; he cannot finish explaining. Their angry exchange ends with stop calling me and fine. She cuts contact. Riped later supplies the unverified death account.

Evidence has limits. A scar proves injury, not who informed. A login proves account use, not sole user. A voice match proves a likely speaker, not a current living location. Independent dates, originals, raw audio, custody marks, rescue records and Riped's corroborating confession establish the complete truth together.

## Characters and performance

Callum Reed is male and aroace. Establish this plainly in the adult service portion of the prologue when Lio and Jess flirt and respect his refusal. He has no romantic partner because he does not want one, not because trauma made him incapable of love. Grevyn's bond with him is deep friendship. Together does not become a romance ending.

Cal experiences anxiety, particularly when Grevyn calls him Cal, which recalls the failed rescue and broken call. Tay and TayJ can comfortably use his nickname. Time apart from them worsens his depression and withdrawal. Show the struggle through sleep, routines, silence and requests for contact; do not turn friends into a required medication or score. They maintain boundaries and independent responsibilities. Cal develops support beyond the pair. Safe pull-offs, grounding, rest and accessible presentation are available without penalties.

Grevyn has agency before, during and after rescue. Her research is her work. She warns the crew during conditioned attacks, requests support, asks about corrections and ultimately chooses severing. Her dangerous abilities arise from supernatural experimentation, coercion and the field; do not equate psychological trauma with inevitable violence. Distinguish lucid, conditioned and intrusion performance states in recording. Her powers do not heal the severe fall injuries. Rehabilitation and accountability persist even in the hopeful ending.

Riped's jealousy concerns being excluded from trust and closeness. Do not require a secret romantic motive. His cockiness and jokes occur before and after the betrayal, but scene direction lets calculation surface in pauses and contradictions. He is Cal's old friend, not Cal's trusted confidant. Tay initially defends him; TayJ doubts him and challenges his stories. Neither is omniscient. His confession confirms evidence rather than conveniently replacing the investigation.

Tay is a warm, protective medic who challenges Cal's self-neglect. TayJ is an observant radio operator and navigator who keeps records and notices inconsistencies. They are high-school friends of Cal and remained close after collapse. Both have work, opinions, rest needs and relationships outside him. Tay's clinical judgement and TayJ's recordkeeping matter equally to rescue and truth.

The cast directory defines ages, voice genders and briefs for sixteen roles. Sable, Ness, Dain, Voss, Merrin, Orin, Lio and Jess are supporting roles supplied in this draft. The Dominion is a working name for the evil force. Road Force's female and Dominion's male voice casting are production choices supplied here; they are separate entities, not alternate aspects. These additions can be renamed without changing established canon, but regenerate manifests before recording.

## Ending rules

Planned survival needs complete corrected research, Sable's review, verified Golghis, stable bodily support, working containment, Grevyn's informed consent and enough effective field capacity. It is deterministic in this design. Input mistakes pause at safe checkpoints rather than secretly killing her.

Only the emergency severing uses chance: proposed production value 65% survival, explicitly spoken and displayed before irrevocable confirmation. This number is an authored balance choice, not an earlier user-selected percentage or a measured medical probability. Test and settle it before voice recording. Persist a single draw at commitment; reloading cannot reroll. There are repeated marked repair opportunities, including M34 and cancellation before M35. Do not hide a last missed ingredient or test friendship to determine life.

Fatal transfer requires verified bodily support for Grevyn and a distinct confirmation that Cal dies. Golghis may still be used for support. Do not offer a death that secretly also fails to save her; first restore the necessary support and show the conditions. Road Force destruction of the transferred bond is supernatural, not a real-world self-harm procedure.

If both survive, Grevyn states that she wants to know Cal now, slowly, and that either proximity or distance can respect that. The player chooses Cal's answer. Together and separate are equal, honest outcomes. Neither automatically restores trust. If Cal dies, Grevyn's grief and future are hers; she owes him no particular life. If Grevyn dies, Cal's grief includes asking for company and further support. No ending implies depression is solved by suffering.

## Gameplay, sound and cinema

Forty missions comprise a four-mission prologue and thirty-six present-day missions. The budget totals 2,580 active minutes / 43 hours, including traversal, repairs, survival and encounters. Do not lengthen it with locked loading screens, idle generators or unskippable travel speech. Tune distances and task density through representative playtests; record actual medians separately from targets.

Use dedicated cinematic tracks. Follow player or vehicle motion from an offset, frame the character receiving news, and insert evidence objects only when discovery warrants it. Cutscene durations are shot minima, not hard stops that cut actors off. Subtitle or voice completion and explicit interaction gates advance dialogue. Gameplay control returns before driving hazards, puzzle choices and any final consent.

Hints progress from useful composition and diegetic clues to an optional voiced suggestion, then a visible interactable marker on request. Never reveal unseen rooms through walls or identify a culprit before earned evidence. Cinematic focus is not object collection. Preserve cab/chase preference, FOV, zoom, input state and reduced-motion settings when a sequence ends.

Original score cues listed in the implementation guide are requests for production, not audio claimed to exist. Ask the owner/composer for these tracks or stems and their permitted use before shipping. Existing menu soundtrack rights are not automatically campaign rights. Dialogue takes precedence over score; preserve engine, footstep, door, ignition, cargo, radio and impact cues without obscuring speech.

Mature language is selective: betrayal, fear, anger and an occasional exasperated repair. No identity slurs. Deaths are non-graphic. Audience-only villain scenes are labelled and do not add facts to Cal's journal before he learns them.

## Recording contract

Every spoken line has a stable LH2 ID. IDs are never reused for changed words after a recording lock; append revision metadata and new take IDs as necessary. Directions and condition labels are not spoken. Each actor receives every line, including branch-only endings, runtime barks, preceding compatible cues and scene context. Men and Women folders reflect role casting, not restrictions on who may audition. Nonverbal effort sessions are separate and consent-directed: breathing, exertion, brief pain, involuntary field strain. No prolonged screaming required.

Recording filename: LINE_ID_take01.wav, replacing LINE_ID with the manifest ID; mono dry 48 kHz/24-bit preferred. Keep the ID and spoken text unchanged. Leave room tone and natural pauses; do not add radio effects to the source performance. Vocal strain should be performed safely, with breaks; postproduction supplies intensity. Pronunciations below are suggested production decisions: Grevyn GREH-vin; Riped RYE-ped; Gucki GUH-kee; Golghis GOHL-giss; Scatterballasian skat-er-buh-LAY-zhun; TayJ TAY-jay. Confirm these with the owner before the first recording session, not midway through takes.
